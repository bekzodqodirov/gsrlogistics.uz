A truck that carries goods across Uzbekistan in transit travels under customs control on an **electronic transit declaration**, filed through the customs E-Tranzit system before or at the border crossing (Customs Code of Uzbekistan, Articles 257-1 and 261; Cabinet of Ministers Resolution No. 737, Regulation points 6 and 8). Where advance information has been submitted, a **TIR Carnet** is accepted as the transit declaration instead (Resolution No. 737, point 2). GSR Logistics is a logistics company in Tashkent, Uzbekistan; since 2018 it has carried cargo from China to Uzbekistan, and it now also handles transit documents for cargo crossing Uzbekistan — its own customs broker files the transit declaration. Below: the documents, the declaration, CMR and TIR, the permit, escort and security, the law’s limits and fees, reloading and the end of transit.

## What is customs transit in Uzbekistan?

In Uzbek customs law, customs transit is a customs regime: goods brought into the country move under customs control across Uzbekistan’s customs territory, between the customs office of departure and the customs office of destination, without payment of customs duties and taxes and without economic-policy measures (Customs Code, Article 116, part one). The customs office of departure is the one in whose zone the carriage under customs control begins; the customs office of destination is the one in whose zone it ends (Customs Code, Article 116, parts two and three).

Transit frees the goods from customs duties and taxes, not from customs fees: customs fees are a customs payment of their own and are charged on transit too (Customs Code, Articles 116 and 289; Cabinet of Ministers Resolution No. 55 of January 31, 2025, annex 1).

## Which documents does a truck need to cross Uzbekistan in transit?

For carriage by road, the transit declaration goes to the customs office of departure with electronic copies of these documents (Cabinet of Ministers Resolution No. 737 of December 31, 2022, Regulation point 13 “a”):

- the vehicle registration certificate (1968 Vienna Convention on Road Traffic);
- the permit for a foreign vehicle, in the cases provided for by Uzbekistan’s international treaties;
- the consignment note under the 1956 CMR Convention;
- the commercial documents;
- documents confirming compliance with prohibitions and restrictions, where the goods may move only with such documents.

The documents are attached to the customs E-Tranzit system through the E-arxiv service; documents attached that way need not be presented again to the state control bodies during customs and other clearance, unless they are of poor quality (blurred, illegible and the like) (Resolution No. 737, Regulation points 9 and 10).

On entry by road, the carrier must present to customs, among other documents, the following (Customs Code, Article 18, point 1):

| Document | What it is | Who presents it |
|---|---|---|
| Vehicle registration certificate | The vehicle’s registration document | The carrier, on entry |
| Permit for a foreign vehicle | Needed where Uzbekistan’s international treaties provide for one | The foreign carrier fills it in before entry (Resolution No. 114, Regulation point 56); the carrier presents it on entry |
| CMR consignment note | Confirms the contract for the international carriage of goods by road | The foreign carrier fills it in before entry (Resolution No. 114, Regulation point 56); the carrier presents it on entry |
| Commercial documents | The invoice, shipping and packing lists and similar (Customs Code, Article 9) | The carrier, on entry |
| TIR Carnet and approval certificate | Where goods travel under TIR Carnets: the carnet and the vehicle’s approval certificate under the TIR Convention | The carrier, on entry |

The documents may be presented in the state language or in other languages used in international carriage (where necessary translated into the state language and certified by the carrier), and on entry customs may not require documents and information other than those Article 18 lists (Customs Code, Article 18, closing parts). ## What is a transit declaration, who files it and when?

A transit declaration is a document submitted to customs, with information on the goods and/or vehicles, used to control goods brought into Uzbekistan and goods moved in transit through it from the customs office of departure to the customs office of destination (Customs Code, Article 257-1, part one). It is submitted in electronic form (Customs Code, Article 257-1, part two), before or at the time the goods and vehicle entering the customs territory cross the customs border (Customs Code, Article 261, part three; Resolution No. 737, Regulation point 6).

The declaring person submits the transit declaration to the customs office of departure through the Customs Committee’s E-Tranzit system, certified with an electronic digital signature (Resolution No. 737, point 2 and Regulation points 3 and 8). The transit declaration may be filed by the declarant in his own name — for road transit the Customs Code names the person carrying the goods (the driver or an authorised person) — or by a customs clearance specialist on the staff of the declarant or of a customs broker that has a contract with the declarant (Resolution No. 737, Regulation point 2; Customs Code, Article 274, point 3). GSR has its own customs broker, so we file the transit declaration ourselves; details are on the page [Transit documents in Uzbekistan](/en/services/transit-documents/).

To fill in and submit the declaration, the filer logs in to E-Tranzit with the electronic digital signature issued to him as an individual and chooses one of four roles (Customs Committee Regulation No. 3382, point 3):

- the carrier or a person acting for it (other than the driver);
- the driver;
- the employee of the customs broker that serves the declarant;
- the consignee or a person acting for it.

Once registered, the transit declaration receives an identification code automatically; when the goods and vehicle reach the customs office of departure, the carrier presents that code together with the accompanying documents (Resolution No. 737, Regulation points 2, 14 and 16). If the goods named in a transit declaration are not presented within thirty calendar days from the day it was registered, the declaration is treated as not filed and is cancelled automatically (Resolution No. 737, Regulation point 6, last paragraph, and Regulation No. 3382, point 60).

One transit declaration covers one consignment, or several consignments only if they travel in one vehicle, under one transport document, from one consignor to one consignee (Resolution No. 737, Regulation point 4; Customs Code, Article 220, part three). The customs fee for clearing goods under the transit regime is 25% of the base calculation amount (BRV) per declaration (Cabinet of Ministers Resolution No. 55, annex 1, position 1 “b”).

Under the Regulation on advance electronic information (Cabinet of Ministers Resolution No. 762 of September 25, 2018, point 2 and Regulation point 3), advance information may be given, for road transport, by the persons (the driver) who actually move the goods and vehicles across the customs border, and also by carriers, owners of the goods or persons with powers over the goods. Advance information is given in the state language, Russian or English, and for road transport it is due in the customs information system one hour before the customs border of Uzbekistan is crossed (Resolution No. 762, Regulation points 6 and 7). When the transit declaration is submitted before the goods and vehicle cross the customs border, the advance electronic information required by legislation does not have to be submitted separately (Resolution No. 737, point 2, paragraph three).

Presidential Decree No. UP-174 of August 27, 2026 provides that from June 1, 2027 a procedure is to be introduced for submitting transit declarations before vehicles reach the border customs post (point 4, item “j”).

## When is no transit declaration needed?

A transit declaration is not required, among other cases (Cabinet of Ministers Resolution No. 737, Regulation point 7):

- for goods and vehicles leaving the country, except goods under the re-export regime;
- for empty vehicles, unless the vehicle itself is brought in or moved in transit as goods;
- for goods carried under a TIR Carnet with advance information submitted;
- for goods carried under an ATA carnet with advance information submitted.

Where advance information has been submitted to customs, a TIR Carnet completed under the 1975 TIR Convention (and an ATA carnet under the 1990 Istanbul Convention) is accepted as the transit declaration (Resolution No. 737, point 2, paragraph two; Customs Code, Article 220, part five). ## CMR and TIR: what is the difference?

In short, the CMR consignment note confirms the contract of carriage, and the TIR Carnet is an international customs document:

| | CMR | TIR |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | The consignment note that confirms the contract for the international carriage of goods by road (CMR Convention of 1956, Articles 1 and 4) | An international customs document that also proves that an international guarantee covers the duties and taxes on the goods (TIR Convention of 1975) |
| Who issues or signs it | Made out in three originals signed by the sender and the carrier: the first for the sender, the second travels with the goods, the third stays with the carrier (CMR Convention, Article 5) | Issued to the carrier by the authorised national association of the carrier’s own country, which also acts as guarantor; under Article 17 of the TIR Convention, one carnet is made out for each road vehicle or container |
| What it does at the border | One of the documents for international road carriage under Uzbek law (Customs Code, Article 18; Resolution No. 737, Regulation point 13 “a”) | Opened in the country of departure, it serves as the customs control document in the countries of departure, transit and destination; in Uzbekistan, with advance information, it is accepted as the transit declaration (Resolution No. 737, point 2) |
| Parties | Uzbekistan, Kazakhstan and Afghanistan are among the parties; China is not | Uzbekistan, Kazakhstan, China and Afghanistan are all parties |

Among other things, a CMR consignment note states the date and place it is made out, the sender, the carrier and the consignee, the place and date of taking over of the goods and the place designated for delivery, the description of the goods, the packages, the gross weight, the charges, the customs instructions and a statement that the carriage is subject to the CMR (CMR Convention, Article 6). The CMR applies to every contract for the carriage of goods by road in vehicles for reward when the place of taking over of the goods and the place designated for delivery, as specified in the contract, are in two different countries, at least one of which is a contracting country (CMR Convention, Article 1); a missing or faulty consignment note does not cancel the contract (Article 4).

Under the TIR Convention, goods carried under TIR pay no import or export duties or taxes, and no deposit, at customs offices en route (Article 4), and goods in sealed vehicles are as a rule not examined there (Article 5); customs may still examine them in exceptional cases. The TIR Convention covers transport “without intermediate reloading” (Article 2). Under Uzbek law, carriage under the TIR Convention is one of the cases in which customs escort is not applied (Customs Code, Article 223).

By resolutions No. 131-I and No. 132-I of August 31, 1995, the Oliy Majlis of Uzbekistan resolved to accede to the 1975 TIR Convention and to the 1956 CMR Convention. ## Does a foreign truck need a permit (dozvol) to cross Uzbekistan?

A foreign truck needs an Uzbekistan permit in the cases provided for by Uzbekistan’s international treaties; where those treaties provide for bilateral or transit carriage without a permit, the carriage is performed on the international consignment note under the CMR Convention (Customs Code, Article 18; Resolution No. 737, Regulation point 13 “a”; Cabinet of Ministers Resolution No. 114, Regulation point 4). The rules are in the Regulation approved by Cabinet of Ministers Resolution No. 114 of March 25, 2026, in force since June 1, 2026: the permit must be filled in before the truck enters Uzbekistan and be carried by the driver, and — unless a treaty or a joint-commission protocol provides otherwise — it may be used once, for one entry and one exit and for one kind of carriage (Regulation points 55–57).

The legal word for the permit a foreign truck may need is “ruxsatnoma” in Uzbek and “разрешение” in Russian; an official text writes “dozvol” in brackets next to it (Cabinet of Ministers Resolution No. 762, annex 2 to the Regulation). A permit is a paper or electronic document giving the right to use a vehicle registered in one state for international carriage on the territory of another; an “Uzbekistan permit” gives that right in Uzbekistan to a vehicle registered abroad, and the authorised body is the Ministry of Transport (Resolution No. 114, Regulation point 2). Permits exist in paper and electronic form; electronic permits are kept in the Ministry of Transport’s E-avto ruxsatnoma information system (Resolution No. 114, Regulation points 2, 61 and 63).

Where a permit is required, its number is entered in the road transit declaration (Customs Committee Regulation No. 3382, point 21, sub-point 8), and the permit must be filled in before entry, carried by the driver and shown to the control authorities on request (Resolution No. 114, Regulation point 56). Unless Uzbekistan’s international treaties provide otherwise, foreign carriers’ vehicles may not carry goods or passengers between points inside Uzbekistan (Resolution No. 114, Regulation point 7).

## When does customs escort a truck, and what is security for customs payments?

For a truck carrying foreign goods, Uzbek customs law provides three ways to secure compliance: customs escort, carriage by a customs carrier, or security for the customs payments (Customs Code, Article 222). Escort is the rule unless an exception applies — for example carriage under a TIR Carnet, or security lodged for the customs payments (Customs Code, Article 223) — and customs may still escort where its risk-management system calls for it (Cabinet of Ministers Resolution No. 800 of December 22, 2020, annex 1, point 9).

Customs escort is the accompanying of road vehicles by the customs authorities under customs control, and under the Customs Code it is subject to a customs fee (Articles 15 and 223). Under Cabinet of Ministers Resolution No. 55 (annex 1, position 5), the escort fee is two base calculation amounts per vehicle for a distance of up to 200 km on Uzbek territory and five base calculation amounts for more than 200 km. Under Articles 235 and 338 of the Customs Code, a customs carrier is an Uzbek legal entity that holds a licence for international carriage of goods by road, has secured customs payments under chapter 47 (2,000 base calculation amounts), has vehicles fit for carriage under customs seals and insures its civil liability; it carries goods under customs control without customs escort.

The Customs Code lists five forms of security for customs payments: a cash deposit with customs, a pledge of goods, a bank guarantee, insurance and surety (Customs Code, Article 339). The security is calculated on the customs payments that would be due if the goods were released for free circulation (Customs Code, Article 337); to free a truck from escort it is lodged electronically, separately for each vehicle, before the carriage begins, and it may not exceed the equivalent of 100,000 euros per vehicle carrying foreign goods, except vehicles carrying live animals (Resolution No. 800, annex 1, points 10–11, and annex 2, point 9). In the road transit declaration the filer selects the method of security from a list; if customs payments are not secured, the entry “customs escort” is selected (Customs Committee Regulation No. 3382, point 31).

## How long may transit take, and what happens if the truck is late?

As of November 12, 2026, the delivery deadline for goods in transit through Uzbekistan is set in each case by the customs office of departure, within two ceilings of the Customs Code — ten calendar days for road transport (Article 218, part four) and 1,000 km per fifteen calendar days for land transport, counted from the day of the transit notification (Article 120, part three) — and on a reasoned request it may be extended by the intermediate customs office (Article 218, part eight). These are legal maximums, not transit times.

As of November 12, 2026, if the carrier misses the delivery deadline set by customs, the customs office at the destination charges the carrier a fee; for road transport it is one base calculation amount for each day of delay (Customs Code, Article 218, part six; Cabinet of Ministers Resolution No. 737 of December 31, 2022, point 3¹, in force since May 4, 2025). If the truck travels under security for customs payments instead of customs escort and the goods do not reach the destination customs post within the set term in unchanged condition, customs can also claim the customs payments on the goods under that security; the goods must then be delivered in full, or the payments made, within ten working days of its demand (Cabinet of Ministers Resolution No. 800 of December 22, 2020, annex 2, points 3, 34 and 35).

The law’s limits and fees, each with its act:

| What | Legal maximum or fee | Act |
|---|---|---|
| Delivery deadline, road transport | Ten calendar days at most | Customs Code, Article 218, part four |
| Term of customs transit, land transport | 1,000 km per fifteen calendar days at most, counted from the day of the transit notification | Customs Code, Article 120, part three |
| Temporary storage on the way | Sixty calendar days at most, ten for perishable goods | Customs Code, Articles 119 and 85 |
| Declaration whose goods are not presented | Cancelled after thirty calendar days from registration | Resolution No. 737, Regulation point 6 |
| Customs fee for a transit declaration | 25% of the base calculation amount (BRV) | Resolution No. 55, annex 1, position 1 “b” |
| Customs escort fee | Two BRV per vehicle up to 200 km, five BRV above 200 km | Resolution No. 55, annex 1, position 5 |
| Late delivery, road transport | One BRV for each day of delay, charged to the carrier | Customs Code, Article 218, part six, and Resolution No. 737, point 3¹ |

*Legal maximums and fees, not transit times; checked against lex.uz on November 12, 2026.*

## Can cargo be reloaded or stored on the way?

Yes, under the control of the intermediate customs office: goods in transit may be transshipped from the vehicle that brought them into Uzbekistan to the vehicle that will take them out (Customs Code, Article 119, part one). Under the same control they may be unloaded and temporarily warehoused — stored, split into consignments or built up into consignments, and similar operations — in places of temporary storage, to suspend the running of the transit term, including where the vehicle is damaged (Customs Code, Article 119, part one).

The carrier determines how long the goods stay, from the time the operations or repairs need, within the maximum of the temporary storage regime: sixty calendar days, and ten for perishable goods (Customs Code, Article 119, part one, and Article 85). The intermediate customs office is the one in whose zone the permitted operations take place, and it may forbid an operation that would make further customs control impossible (Customs Code, Article 119, parts two and three).

Cargo operations with goods carried under customs control take place in places designated for them and in customs working hours, elsewhere or outside working hours only on a written application and under customs control; customs may refuse only if an operation could cause loss of the goods, change their properties or make further control impossible; after transshipment, the carrier that accepted the goods for onward carriage answers for the rules (Customs Code, Article 225). In carriage by road, goods under customs control may not share a vehicle with goods that are not under customs control (Customs Code, Article 218, part nine).

## How does transit end?

The transit regime ends when the goods are taken out of the customs territory (Customs Code, Article 120, part one). The authorised person must present the goods and the documents of Article 18 to the customs office of destination within the term set by the customs office of departure, and the customs office of destination carries out the operations that complete the regime (Customs Code, Article 120, parts four and five).

Once the carrier has delivered the goods and vehicle and the fees calculated at the border customs post are paid, the delivery is confirmed in the customs information system, and with that confirmation the transit regime is completed (Cabinet of Ministers Resolution No. 737, Regulation points 5, 21 and 22). Where Uzbekistan’s international treaties provide for customs marks on the accompanying documents, the customs office of destination puts the mark and returns the documents to the carrier (Resolution No. 737, Regulation point 23).

On the authorised person’s application the regime may also be completed at an intermediate customs office, by taking the goods out in separate consignments or through other customs offices of destination, or by placing them under another customs regime (Customs Code, Article 120, part six).
