The price of cargo from China comes down to three things: the mode (truck, air or rail), the chargeable weight (actual or volumetric, whichever is greater) and the density in kg/m³. As of September 2026, truck cargo costs roughly $6.50–7.50/kg, or $110–130/m³ for light goods, and air $9–12/kg. Below are the formulas, the 170 kg/m³ rule, 5 worked examples and what the price includes.

## What makes up the cargo price?

Cargo is delivery from a warehouse in China to a warehouse in Tashkent at a single price. That price usually covers four jobs: intake and measuring at the Yiwu warehouse, consolidation (joining a lot), transport via Khorgos, and customs clearance in Tashkent. What moves the price:

1. **Mode.** Truck is the cheapest at 15–25 days; air is the fastest at 5–10 days but $9–12 per kilogram; rail is priced per container and takes 20–35 days.
2. **Chargeable weight.** Not the weight on the scale, but the greater of actual and volumetric weight.
3. **Density.** Heavy, dense cargo is cheaper per kilogram; light, bulky cargo is cheaper per cubic metre.
4. **Goods category.** By air, branded and serial goods (3 or more identical units) cost more; goods with batteries, liquids and magnets travel by truck only.
5. **Extra services.** Inspection, repacking, insurance and regional delivery are charged separately.

## What is volumetric weight and how is it calculated?

Volumetric weight is a notional "weight" derived from the box dimensions. Space in a truck or aircraft is limited: a large box weighing 12 kg takes more room than a small one weighing 25 kg, so the carrier counts volume as well. The formula:

| Mode | Formula | How many kg one m³ counts as |
|---|---|---|
| Truck cargo | length × width × height (cm) ÷ 6 000 | ≈ 167 kg |
| Air cargo | length × width × height (cm) ÷ 5 000 | 200 kg |

Chargeable weight = the greater of actual and volumetric. Example: a 50×40×40 cm box weighing 8 kg. Volumetric weight: 80,000 ÷ 5 000 = 16 kg (air) or 80,000 ÷ 6 000 = 13.3 kg (truck). By air you pay for 16 kg, because 16 > 8. Almost every carrier on the Tashkent market applies the same rule: ÷ 6 000 for truck, ÷ 5 000 for air (Transasia, June 23, 2026).

## What is density, and when is cargo priced per kg or per m³?

Density is the ratio of cargo weight to its volume, in kg/m³. Calculation: total weight ÷ total volume. 300 kg and 2 m³ → 150 kg/m³; 48 kg and 0.2 m³ → 240 kg/m³.

The truck cargo rule: **density above 170 kg/m³ is priced per kilogram, below it per cubic metre.** The number 170 is no accident: under the ÷ 6 000 formula one cubic metre holds about 167 kg, so above that threshold the actual weight exceeds the volumetric weight. Air is always priced per kilogram.

Typical densities: tightly packed clothing 250–350 kg/m³, boxed footwear 150–200, fabric on rolls 350–500, plastic toys 60–100, furniture 80–150, spare parts and metal goods 400–700. So clothing and spare parts go per kilogram; toys and furniture go per cubic metre.

## 5 examples: how the price comes out

**Example 1. Clothing, truck.** 3 boxes of 60×40×40 cm, 85 kg in total. Volume: 3 × 0.096 = 0.288 m³. Density: 85 ÷ 0.288 = 295 kg/m³ → per kg. The 30–100 kg tariff: $7/kg. Price: 85 × 7 = **$595**.

**Example 2. Plastic toys, truck.** 1.2 m³, 120 kg. Density: 100 kg/m³ → per m³. Tariff $110/m³. Price: 1.2 × 110 = **$132**. Per kilogram it would have been 120 × 6.50 = $780 — which is exactly why light cargo is priced per cubic metre.

**Example 3. Fabric, wholesale, truck.** 400 kg, 1 m³. Density 400 kg/m³ → per kg. Standard tariff $6.50/kg → $2,600; dense wholesale tariff from $3.50/kg → from $1,400. The final figure is confirmed after weighing: **$1,400–2,600**.

**Example 4. Electronics, air.** A 50×40×40 cm box, 8 kg. Volumetric weight: 80,000 ÷ 5 000 = 16 kg > 8 kg → you pay for 16 kg. Standard goods $9/kg → **$144**; branded $12/kg → $192.

**Example 5. Samples: air or truck?** A 40×40×30 cm box, 20 kg. Air: volumetric weight 48,000 ÷ 5 000 = 9.6 kg < 20 → 20 × 9 = **$180**, 5–10 days. Truck: density 20 ÷ 0.048 = 417 kg/m³ → per kg, 20 × 7.50 = **$150**, 15–25 days. A $30 difference — for urgent samples, air pays for itself.

## Are customs payments included in the price?

Customs clearance — documents, HS code, GTD — is our job and is included. Duty and VAT are paid to the state and depend on the goods code, so they are not part of the cargo tariff; we calculate them at the quote stage.

- **Legal entities and sole traders:** duty 0–30% (clothing 20%, phones 5%, furniture 15%, computers 0%), VAT 12% on customs value plus duty, and a customs fee of 1 BRV for lots up to $10,000. For example, a $5,000 clothing lot: duty $1,000, VAT (5,000 + 1,000) × 12% = $720 — about $1,720 in total plus 1 BRV.
- **Individuals:** since May 1, 2025 the courier-shipment limit is $200 per month, with 30% on the excess and a minimum of $3/kg (Cabinet of Ministers Resolution No. 244). Every shipment also carries a fee of 2% of BRV per kilogram (Resolution No. 55, from May 4, 2025). As Gazeta.uz reported on September 2, 2026, the rate becomes 20% and $2/kg from January 1, 2027.

Goods for a shop or a marketplace are commercial cargo; they cannot be brought in "duty-free", and we make no such promise. Details: [Customs clearance](/en/services/customs/).

## How can you lower the shipping cost?

1. **Increase the density.** Ask the supplier for vacuum packing or a smaller box; repacking at the warehouse costs $0.40/kg — shrinking a box from 60×50×40 to 50×40×35 cm means paying for 12 kg instead of 20 by truck.
2. **Combine lots.** 14 days of free storage in China is enough to gather deliveries from three or four sellers into one lot; from 100 kg the tariff drops to $6.50/kg.
3. **Pick the right mode.** Light bulky goods — truck per m³; dense heavy goods — truck per kg; lots above 15 m³ — a container.
4. **Mind the holidays.** Before Chinese New Year and October 1–7, prices and transit times rise; around November 11, on the contrary, there are discounts.
5. **Prepare documents early.** When the invoice and the HS code match, the cargo does not sit at customs — no storage or demurrage costs.
