The price of cargo from China comes down to three things: the mode (truck, air or rail), the volume of the cargo or its chargeable weight, and the density in kg/m³. As of September 2026, truck cargo starts at $110 per m³ — the density picks the exact rate — and air costs $9–12/kg. Below are the formulas, the density table, 5 worked examples and what the price includes.

## What makes up the cargo price?

Cargo is delivery from a warehouse in China to a warehouse in Tashkent at a single price. That price usually covers four jobs: intake and measuring at the Yiwu warehouse, consolidation (joining a lot), transport via Khorgos, and customs clearance in Tashkent. What moves the price:

1. **Mode.** Truck is the cheapest at 15–25 days; air is the fastest at 5–10 days but $9–12 per kilogram; rail is priced per container and takes 20–35 days.
2. **Volume and chargeable weight.** By truck you pay for the real volume of the cargo (m³); by air, for the greater of actual and volumetric weight.
3. **Density.** For truck cargo it sets the price of a cubic metre: from $110 for light goods to $320 for dense ones.
4. **Goods category.** By air, branded and serial goods (3 or more identical units) cost more; goods with batteries, liquids and magnets travel by truck only.
5. **Extra services.** Inspection, repacking, insurance and regional delivery are charged separately.

## What is volumetric weight and how is it calculated?

Volumetric weight is a notional "weight" derived from the box dimensions. Space in an aircraft is limited: a large box weighing 12 kg takes more room than a small one weighing 25 kg, so the air carrier counts volume as well. Truck cargo is billed on the volume itself, so there it needs no volumetric weight:

| Mode | What you pay for | Formula |
|---|---|---|
| Truck cargo | volume (m³), with the density picking the rate | length × width × height (cm) ÷ 1 000 000 = m³; minimum 1 m³ — nothing smaller is accepted |
| Air cargo | chargeable weight (kg) | the greater of the actual weight and length × width × height (cm) ÷ 5 000 |

Example: a 50×40×40 cm box weighing 8 kg. By air the volumetric weight is 80,000 ÷ 5 000 = 16 kg, and 16 > 8, so you pay for 16 kg. By truck the same box is 0.08 m³ — under 1 m³, so it is not accepted by truck on its own: the truck price follows the volume and the density of a shipment of at least 1 m³. The ÷ 6 000 rule (1 m³ ≈ 167 kg) is still used on the market to judge how bulky a shipment is (Transasia, June 23, 2026), but it does not set our truck tariff.

## What is density, and how does it set the truck price?

Density is the ratio of cargo weight to its volume, in kg/m³. Calculation: total weight ÷ total volume. 180 kg and 2 m³ → 90 kg/m³; 240 kg and 1 m³ → 240 kg/m³.

Truck cargo is priced from exactly that number: **the density picks the rate for one cubic metre, and that rate is multiplied by the volume.** The denser the cargo, the more a cubic metre costs — the same space carries more weight.

| Density | Price per m³ |
|---|---|
| up to 100 kg/m³ | $110 |
| 101–150 kg/m³ | $130 |
| 151–200 kg/m³ | $160 |
| 201–250 kg/m³ | $180 |
| 251–300 kg/m³ | $200 |
| 301–350 kg/m³ | $230 |
| 351–400 kg/m³ | $260 |
| 401–450 kg/m³ | $280 |
| 451–500 kg/m³ | $290 |
| 501–700 kg/m³ | $300 |
| 701–1,000 kg/m³ | $320 |
| 1,000 kg/m³ and above | $0.55/kg |

The same two examples in money: 240 kg in 1 m³ → 240 kg/m³ → 1 × 180 = $180; 180 kg in 2 m³ → 90 kg/m³ → 2 × 110 = $220. Cargo denser than water — 1,200 kg in 1 m³, that is 1,200 kg/m³ — is billed per kilogram: 1,200 × 0.55 = $660, because such a load runs out the truck's tonnage before its volume. Consolidated truck cargo is accepted from 1 m³ — we do not carry anything smaller. The 1 m³ counts across one shipment: pieces from different sellers are gathered at the Yiwu receiving point under one GS code. Cargo under 1 m³ can go by air, from 0.5 kg. Air is always priced per kilogram.

Typical densities and the rate they fall into:

| Goods | Typical density | Truck rate |
|---|---|---|
| Plastic toys | 60–100 kg/m³ | $110/m³ |
| Furniture | 80–150 kg/m³ | $110–130/m³ |

| Fabric on rolls | 350–500 kg/m³ | $230–290/m³ |

## 5 examples: how the price comes out

**Example 1. Clothing, truck.** 12 boxes of 60×40×40 cm, 340 kg in total. Volume: 12 × 0.096 = 1.152 m³. Density: 340 ÷ 1.152 = 295 kg/m³ → the 251–300 band, $200/m³. Price: 1.152 × 200 = **$230.40**.

**Example 2. Plastic toys, truck.** 3.6 m³, 360 kg. Density: 360 ÷ 3.6 = 100 kg/m³ → $110/m³. Price: 3.6 × 110 = **$396**. The same 360 kg packed into 1.2 m³: 300 kg/m³ → $200/m³ → 1.2 × 200 = $240 — the volume shrinks threefold while the rate less than doubles.

**Example 3. Fabric, wholesale, truck.** 400 kg, 1 m³. Density: 400 ÷ 1 = 400 kg/m³ → the 351–400 band, $260/m³. Price: 1 × 260 = **$260**. The same 400 kg loosely stacked over 2 m³: 200 kg/m³ → $160/m³ → 2 × 160 = $320. The final figure is confirmed after measuring at the warehouse.

**Example 4. Electronics, air.** A 50×40×40 cm box, 8 kg. Volumetric weight: 80,000 ÷ 5 000 = 16 kg > 8 kg → you pay for 16 kg. Standard goods $9/kg → **$144**; branded $12/kg → $192.

**Example 5. Samples: air or truck?** A 40×40×30 cm box, 20 kg. Air: volumetric weight 48,000 ÷ 5 000 = 9.6 kg < 20 → 20 × 9 = **$180**, 5–10 days. Truck: volume 0.048 m³ — under 1 m³, so this box is not accepted by truck. Shipped on its own, the box therefore goes by air only — provided it contains no batteries, magnets, liquids, cosmetics or perfume: those cannot fly, and under 1 m³ they cannot be shipped at all.

## Are customs payments included in the price?

Customs clearance — documents, HS code, GTD — is our job and is included. Duty and VAT are paid to the state and depend on the goods code, so they are not part of the cargo tariff; we calculate them at the quote stage.

- **Legal entities and sole traders:** duty 0–30% (clothing 20%, phones 5%, furniture 15%, computers 0%), VAT 12% on customs value plus duty, and a customs fee of 1 BRV for lots up to $10,000. For example, a $5,000 clothing lot: duty $1,000, VAT (5,000 + 1,000) × 12% = $720 — about $1,720 in total plus 1 BRV.
- **Individuals:** since May 1, 2025 the courier-shipment limit is $200 per month, with 30% on the excess and a minimum of $3/kg (Cabinet of Ministers Resolution No. 244). Every shipment also carries a fee of 2% of BRV per kilogram (Resolution No. 55, from May 4, 2025). As Gazeta.uz reported on September 2, 2026, the rate becomes 20% and $2/kg from January 1, 2027.

Goods for a shop or a marketplace are commercial cargo; they cannot be brought in "duty-free", and we make no such promise. Details: [Customs clearance](/en/services/customs/).

## How can you lower the shipping cost?

1. **Increase the density.** Ask the supplier for vacuum packing or a smaller box: 240 kg of soft goods in 4 m³ is 60 kg/m³ → $110/m³ → $440, while the same 240 kg compressed into 1.6 m³ is 150 kg/m³ → $130/m³ → $208. Repacking at the warehouse costs $0.40/kg ($96 here), so the net saving is $136.
2. **Combine lots.** The 1 m³ minimum applies to the whole shipment: pieces from different sellers are gathered at the Yiwu receiving point under one GS code and counted together. So gather purchases that are each under 1 m³ into one shipment — separately they are not accepted by truck; once together they reach 1 m³, they travel by truck, the cheapest mode. The 14 days of free storage in China exist for exactly this.
3. **Pick the right mode.** Light bulky goods and dense goods both travel by truck and are both priced per m³ — only at 1,000 kg/m³ and above does the tariff flip to $0.55/kg; compare a lot above 15 m³ with a container; take air when the deadline decides or the cargo is under 1 m³.
4. **Mind the holidays.** Before Chinese New Year and October 1–7, prices and transit times rise; around November 11, on the contrary, there are discounts.
5. **Prepare documents early.** When the invoice and the HS code match, the cargo does not sit at customs — no storage or demurrage costs.
